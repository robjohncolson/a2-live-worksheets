=== Field 1 ===

LEHS celebrates its diversity, with over 90% of students identifying as Hispanic, Asian, African 
American, or mixed race. Approximately. 41% of the students are multilingual learners and 
73% of the students speak another language as their first language. This diversity is embraced 
through clubs, teams, activities, and, most importantly, in classrooms. Culturally responsive 
teaching is a key focus, with materials that reflect the student body and a district commitment 
to hiring a more diverse staff.
LEHS has a student body that is 41% multilingual learners, 13% students with disabilities, 82% 
high needs, and 75% economically disadvantaged. As illustrated on multiple occasions, 
including the recent NEASC visit and walkthroughs, the staff is deeply dedicated to student 
success, with teachers, administrators, and support staff demonstrating an unwavering 
commitment to supporting every learner. 
Over 95% of eligible students participated in the MCAS exam this past year, with strong gains 
in Mathematics and ELA. While analysis shows students continue to struggle with certain 
question types and topics, the gains in growth were notable. 
Data reveals school-wide progress in ACCESS scores, the 4-year graduation rate, and school 
belonging. Chronic absenteeism continues to be a significant challenge, and the school 
remains committed to implementing strategies that foster consistent student attendance and 
engagement.
The Newcomer Academy and Honors ESL classes, which were designed to meet the unique 
needs of first- and second-year English Learner students, have proven successful. ACCESS 
data shows much higher scores among these students than with non-academy and nonhonors peers. The school also began the implementation of a writing protocol in ELA and the 
continuation of a Math Lab, both of which are intended to provide mechanisms for students to 
improve core academic skills.
Interventions in place include:
• Weekly and monthly PLCs
• Instructional Leadership Team
• BPE Support
• MCAS review sessions in Math, ELA, and Science
• Math and ELA labs
• Newcomer Academy 
• DAWGS writing protocol

=== Field 2 ===

By the end of SY 2026–27, students in my identified classes will demonstrate growth in overall 
academic proficiency, such as reasoning, academic discourse, academic language 
proficiency, and completion of rigorous, grade-level tasks. I will document growth for my 
identified student group across at least three times throughout the year, using classrooms 
assignments, student-work samples, and/or structured discussion records. The first 
collection will establish students’ starting performance, and subsequent collections will 
measure progress using consistent rubrics or department-developed criteria. I will use 
language objectives, structured discussion, and appropriate scaffolds so multilingual 
learners and students with disabilities can access and demonstrate grade-level learning.
By June 2027, 70% of students in my identified mathematics classes will improve their scores 
by at least 10 percentage points from their baseline score and/or achieve proficiency (70% or 
higher) on at least three of four standards-aligned unit assessments administered during the 
school year. Student progress will be monitored through unit assessments and analysis of 
student work. To support student growth, I will implement targeted interventions, structured 
mathematical discourse, and scaffolded problem-solving opportunities aligned to grade-level 
standards. Additional supports can include teacher night back, math lab student support, and 
IXL skills Practice.

LPS is committed to improving learning outcomes for all students, including special 
populations such as multilingual learners and students with disabilities. This commitment is

specifically domains 3 and 4 of the instructional core. School-based teams, including an 
Instructional Leadership Team and MTSS team, work together to support the School 
Improvement Plan (SIP). 
Lynn English High School's SIP focus areas are:
• Focus Area 1: Strengthen the instructional core 
• Focus Area 2: Build a coherent professional learning system 
• Focus Area 3: Establish shared accountability through data cycles

=== Field 4 ===

I will engage in collaborative professional learning to plan lessons aligned with Domains 3 and 
4, examine student work, and adjust instruction based on evidence of student learning. 
Through PLC meetings and other collaborative opportunities, I will develop and refine gradelevel tasks, language objectives, opportunities for students to explain their thinking, and 
appropriate supports for multilingual learners and students with disabilities. I will bring 
relevant lesson plans and student work to these discussions, implement agreed-upon 
instructional adjustments, and use walkthrough and observation feedback to strengthen my 
practice.
Possible evidence
• Baseline, midyear, and end-of-year student work scored using consistent criteria.
• Common assessments and progress monitoring records.
• Lesson plans showing grade-level tasks, language objectives, and planned scaffolds.
• PLC records documenting findings, instructional adjustments, and follow-up results.
• Student work samples collected before and after instructional adjustments
• Examples of questioning strategies that promote student explanation and reasoning
• Examples of meaningful feedback and students’ subsequent revisions.
• Photos or artifacts of student collaboration and academic discourse structures
• Walkthrough and observation feedback, with evidence of implementation.
